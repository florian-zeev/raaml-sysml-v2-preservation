# Third-party rights audit

**Audit date:** July 31, 2026
**Status:** Conservative source-only boundary selected; limited excerpt review
and hosted-output cleanup remain public-release blockers
**Purpose:** Engineering publication review, not legal advice

## Result

The repository does not currently commit the official OMG XMI files,
specification PDFs, the SysML v2 Pilot Implementation archive, or the Temurin
JDK archives. A review of every path in Git history found no such upstream
binary or standards artifact.

That is necessary, but it is not sufficient. The reproducibility package
generated by the project contains:

- raw and canonical fact extractions from the 17 official RAAML definitions;
- the complete generated SysML v2 representation;
- preservation manifests containing source facts;
- 17 reconstructed SysML v1/UML XMI files;
- comparison reports that include source and reconstructed facts.

These outputs preserve RAAML names, relationships, comments, OCL expressions,
and other content from the official definitions. They may therefore reproduce
copyrightable parts of the source material even though the original XMI bytes
are not included.

The project has selected the conservative route: it must not publish that
generated package unless its redistribution status is later resolved. CI may
generate and compare it inside an ephemeral job, but the workflow does not
retain it as a downloadable artifact.

The narrower route does not by itself answer whether the limited RAAML names,
relationships, aggregate counts, source locations, and hashes used in the
proposal, transformation analysis, and oracle require additional permission
or notices. That question still needs written clarification, qualified legal
review, or a deliberate further reduction before public visibility.

### Upstream terms

The official RAAML 1.1 specification includes a license permitting creation
and distribution of software and special-purpose specifications based on the
specification, subject to stated conditions. It also restricts copying,
posting, modification, and commercial transfer of the specification itself.

OMG's current legal page says that use of OMG copyrighted material outside
its stated guidelines requires permission, and directs requests for use to
OMG in writing. This page was rechecked on July 31, 2026; the engineering
audit does not interpret whether a particular use is permitted by law.

Primary sources:

- [OMG legal notices and copyright permission](https://www.omg.org/legal/)
- [Official RAAML 1.1 specification page](https://www.omg.org/spec/RAAML/1.1/About-RAAML)
- [Official RAAML 1.1 specification PDF](https://www.omg.org/spec/RAAML/1.1/PDF)

The SysML v2 Pilot Implementation is published under EPL-2.0. Its downloaded
archive is used as a validator and is not redistributed by this project:

- [SysML v2 Pilot Implementation](https://github.com/Systems-Modeling/SysML-v2-Pilot-Implementation)

## Optional permission request to OMG

Written clarification is one way to close the remaining source-grounded
material question and could also permit a broader future package. Suggested
text:

```text
Subject: Permission request — RAAML 1.1 / SysML v2 preservation proposal

Hello,

I am preparing an independent, non-normative community proposal and open
reference implementation for preserving the 17 normative RAAML 1.1 profile
and library definitions through a SysML v2 representation.

The public repository would not contain the official OMG XMI files or
specification PDFs. It would contain independently written transformation
rules, schemas, validation software, aggregate results, and a small number of
source-grounded verification samples. Users would obtain official inputs from
their OMG URLs and verify them against recorded hashes.

The implementation can also generate a canonical fact representation, a
SysML v2 rendering, preservation manifests, and reconstructed XMI. These
outputs preserve declaration names, relationships, comments, and OCL
expressions from the official RAAML definitions.

Could you please confirm:

1. whether the proposal, transformation rules, schemas, and small verification
   samples may be published, and the exact copyright and permission notice
   that should accompany them;
2. whether the complete generated fact set, SysML v2 rendering, manifests,
   and reconstructed XMI may be distributed as release artifacts;
3. if the complete generated outputs may not be distributed, whether the
   open-source implementation may generate them locally from official files
   obtained by each user from OMG;
4. whether any additional attribution, trademark language, or license
   boundary is required.

The proposal will state clearly that it is independent, is not an OMG
specification or submission, and does not claim OMG endorsement.

Thank you,
Florian Wolf
```

## Conservative publication boundary

The intended public repository contains the original paper, mapping design,
schemas, source code, provenance lock, aggregate validation counts, and
instructions. The complete fact sets, generated v2 model, manifests, and
reconstructed XMI remain local outputs produced only after each user obtains
the official inputs. Public visibility still waits for the two manual blockers
identified above; this document does not provide legal advice or clearance.
